# Meditation for Fidgety Skeptics
**WWDC21 · Session 10316** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10316/)

_Platforms:_ N/A (keynote/wellness session)

## Overview
This session is a developer wellness talk, not a technical session. Emmy Award-winning ABC News journalist and anchor Dan Harris recounts how a nationally-televised panic attack in 2004 set him on a path to discover mindfulness meditation, ultimately leading him to write the bestselling book "Ten Percent Happier" and co-author "Meditation for Fidgety Skeptics" with renowned meditation teacher Jeff Warren.

Dan shares the three primary benefits he found from meditation: increased calm, improved focus, and mindfulness — the skill of noticing thoughts and emotions without automatically acting on them. He places meditation within a broader "pantheon of no-brainers" for developer well-being, alongside sleep, exercise, healthy eating, access to nature, and quality relationships.

Jeff Warren then leads a short guided meditation session and both speakers address practical advice for building a meditation habit, managing social anxiety post-pandemic, and developing a healthier relationship with technology.

## Key Topics

**Mindfulness Defined**
The ability to notice what is happening in your mind and body at any given moment without automatically being carried away by it — creating the space to respond wisely rather than react blindly.

**Three Core Benefits of Meditation**
1. Calmer baseline — cumulative effect of daily practice, not instant calm
2. Improved focus — the "bicep curl for the brain" model of repeatedly returning attention to one object
3. Mindfulness — observing thoughts/urges without acting on them reflexively

**Common Misconceptions**
The biggest barrier is the belief that correct meditation requires clearing the mind. In reality, the practice is the repeated act of noticing distraction and returning — which is what creates measurable changes in brain structure (attention regulation areas).

**Building a Habit**
- Start with just one minute; do it on 25 out of 31 days ("daily-ish" approach)
- "One minute counts" — lower bar increases success
- Psychological flexibility (the ability to restart without self-judgment) is essential

**Guided Meditation (Jeff Warren)**
A short, five-minute guided session using a developer-oriented metaphor of "layers" and "drop-down menus" to describe how attention moves between different sensory and cognitive layers, with an emphasis on equanimity ("letting everything be here") and good-naturedness.

**Wellness Pantheon for Developers**
Dan identifies five areas alongside meditation: sleep, exercise, healthy eating (without obsession), access to nature, and quality of relationships. He cites evidence that social isolation drives spikes in anxiety, depression, addiction, and suicide, and emphasizes that humans are fundamentally social.

**Managing Technology Use**
Jeff recommends frequent pauses to check in with the nervous system, limiting screen time, and using movement breaks (walking, stretching) as a counterbalance to technology obsession.

## Takeaways
- Meditation is a trainable skill, not a talent; the core practice is noticing distraction and returning, not achieving a blank mind.
- Start small ("one minute counts," "daily-ish") — habit formation is hard and psychological flexibility to restart is more important than consistency.
- Mental states such as calm, focus, and connection are trainable through meditation, and the neuroscience supports this in the form of measurable brain changes.
- Developer well-being encompasses sleep, exercise, nutrition, nature, and relationships in addition to mindfulness — all of which contribute to greater effectiveness and availability for others.
