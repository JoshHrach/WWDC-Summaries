# Principles of great design
**WWDC26 · Session 250** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/250/)

_Platforms:_ All Apple platforms (design session)

## Overview
This session presents eight foundational design principles that Apple's design team applies across all platforms and products. Rather than prescribing specific UI patterns, it articulates the values and intentions that distinguish great design from merely functional design — and argues that these principles apply equally to code, content, and visual decisions.

The session opens with the claim that design is "making something with intention," and that choosing what *not* to build is as important as what you build. It closes with a note that delight — the quality people associate with beloved apps — is not a confetti animation added at the end, but the natural result of getting all the other principles right.

The companion Human Interface Guidelines page "Design principles" (linked in Resources) expands on this material with written guidance.

## Key Topics

### Purpose
Design experiences that genuinely serve people. Respect and adapt to users' lives, be clear and considered in every decision, and ask before writing code: "Does this feature have real purpose? Will people truly value it?"

### Agency
Put people in control. Let them explore freely, avoid forcing predetermined paths, build in forgiveness — easy undo, confirmation for destructive actions — so users feel confident to try things.

### Responsibility
Respect privacy as a human right. Ask only for necessary permissions. Anticipate how features could cause harm, especially with AI. Safeguard people from bad outcomes, and be willing to remove features when risks outweigh value.

### Familiarity
Use metaphors that are neither too literal nor too abstract. Follow established platform conventions for common actions. Maintain consistency: things that look the same should work the same.

### Flexibility
Adapt to the many different contexts, devices, and abilities of users. Design for each platform's strengths. Consider the full range of your audience. Let people personalize when no single solution fits everyone.

### Simplicity
Remove friction — not necessarily elements. Concise language, strong visual hierarchy, and clear affordances help people find what they need. Every element should earn its place; sometimes adding context makes an interface simpler.

### Craft
Uncompromising attention to detail. Beautiful typography, responsive animations, solid performance, and a commitment to iteration show users you care. Great design has longevity: keep evolving it as platforms and needs change.

### Delight
Not confetti or decoration — delight is the natural result of getting everything else right. Identify the emotion you want users to feel, then reinforce it throughout the design. Combine all principles with genuine care.

## Takeaways
- Great design starts with purpose: before writing code, verify the feature genuinely serves users and earns their time and trust.
- Agency and responsibility are inseparable: give users control and protect them from harm, including from your AI features.
- Familiarity and flexibility are complementary: follow conventions so users have a head start, then adapt for each context and ability.
- Delight is earned, not added — it emerges naturally when purpose, craft, simplicity, and the other principles are all in place.
