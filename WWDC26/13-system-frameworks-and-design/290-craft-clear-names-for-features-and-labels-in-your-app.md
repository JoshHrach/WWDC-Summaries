# Craft clear names for features and labels in your app
**WWDC26 · Session 290** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/290/)

_Platforms:_ All Apple platforms (design session)

## Overview
The names used for features, buttons, menu items, and tab labels directly determine whether people understand what an app does and how to navigate it. This session explores the principles behind intentional naming in app design, drawing on patterns observed across Apple's own apps to illustrate what makes a name clear, trustworthy, and useful.

The session presents naming not as a copywriting afterthought but as a core design decision that shapes perception, reduces cognitive load, and builds trust. Good names describe outcomes from the user's perspective (not the system's), are specific enough to distinguish similar actions, and are consistent across the app and platform.

## Key Topics

### Naming as Design
Names are not labels added after design is done — they are design decisions that affect comprehension, navigation, and trust. The time spent choosing a name is an investment in the clarity of the entire experience.

### Principles Behind Thoughtful Naming
- **Describe outcomes, not mechanisms**: users care about what will happen, not how the system implements it. "Save to Library" is clearer than "Write to Store."
- **Be specific**: vague names like "More" or "Options" force users to explore to understand. Specific names reduce hesitation.
- **Maintain consistency**: the same action should have the same name everywhere in the app and align with platform conventions. Inconsistency signals unreliability.
- **Use plain language**: avoid jargon, technical terms, and brand-invented vocabulary unless they are genuinely established in the user's mental model.
- **Match the user's vocabulary**: names should reflect how users describe their own tasks, not how engineers describe the feature.

### Patterns from Apple Apps
The session examines naming patterns across Apple's app portfolio to illustrate these principles in practice — how tab names, action button labels, menu items, and feature names were chosen and what makes them work.

### Naming with Intention
The closing message: every name is a choice, and choosing with intention — asking who uses this, what they want to accomplish, and whether the name makes the answer immediately clear — is the discipline that separates good naming from poor naming.

## Takeaways
- Names are design decisions — invest in them with the same rigor applied to layout and color.
- Describe outcomes from the user's perspective, not the system's implementation.
- Consistency across the app and with platform conventions reduces cognitive load and builds trust.
- Plain, specific language aligned to the user's own vocabulary outperforms creative or technical naming in every context.
