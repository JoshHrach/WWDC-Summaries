# A Conversation with the Cast and Creators of Mythic Quest: Raven's Banquet
**WWDC20 · Session 10969** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10969/)

_Platforms:_ All Platforms

## Overview
This WWDC20 special session features an informal Q&A with the cast and creators of Apple TV+'s hit show Mythic Quest: Raven's Banquet. The session is hosted by Rob McElhenney (executive producer/co-creator, plays Ian Grimm) alongside Charlotte Nicdao (Poppy), Imani Hakim (Dana), Danny Pudi (Brad), and Megan Ganz (executive producer/co-creator). Questions were submitted by developers from the Apple community worldwide.

The conversation covers how the show was researched and developed with guidance from Ubisoft, what it was like for the cast to film the COVID-19 quarantine episode using iPhones, and thoughts on storytelling, the game industry, and developer culture. The session opens with a clip from the show's quarantine episode before transitioning to the live Q&A.

A notable technical discussion centers on the specific tools used to film the quarantine episode entirely on iPhones — including Filmic Pro, MOTIV Audio, and Shure hardware — and how the production team coordinated remotely via Zoom and AirPods. Megan Ganz also encourages aspiring filmmakers to start creating now using the iPhone hardware they already own.

## Key Topics
- **Research and authenticity** — Ubisoft served as the primary partner, sending consultants from multiple studios (programmers, writers, monetization experts) to advise the writers' room. The show aimed to accurately depict developer culture after earlier TV portrayals frustrated real practitioners.
- **Quarantine episode production** — The entire episode was shot by cast members in their own homes using iPhones, coordinated remotely via Zoom and AirPods. Apple provided the equipment including iPhones, lighting, and sound gear.
- **iPhone filmmaking tools** — Specific apps and hardware used: Filmic Pro v6.10.9 (camera), MOTIV Audio v2.62 (audio), Shure MV88 Plus (microphone hardware). Combined these produced footage equivalent to professional on-set cameras.
- **Storytelling advice** — Cast members encouraged aspiring creators (especially a 15-year-old from Germany) to find community, practice storytelling continuously, make mistakes early, and study the craft across formats.
- **Women in gaming** — Ganz discussed being surprised by the large proportion of women playing and making video games, and how marketing had obscured this reality for years.
- **Developer culture observations** — Charlotte Nicdao highlighted the tension in the Poppy/Ian dynamic: Poppy builds everything, yet Ian is credited as the genius — reflecting real dynamics observed in the industry.

## Takeaways
- The iPhone, combined with apps like Filmic Pro and MOTIV Audio, can produce professional-grade film and television content — removing nearly all technical barriers to filmmaking.
- Aspiring creators should start making content now rather than waiting for professional opportunities; iterating on mistakes early builds lasting skills.
- Authentic depiction of developer and gaming culture requires direct collaboration with industry practitioners, not assumptions from prior media.
- Remote production workflows using iPhones, Zoom, and AirPods proved viable for professional episodic television during COVID-19.
