# A Conversation with Lisa Jackson and Former Attorney General Eric Holder
**WWDC20 · Session 10970** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10970/)

_Platforms:_ All Platforms

## Overview
This WWDC20 special session features a wide-ranging conversation between Apple's VP of Environment, Policy and Social Initiatives, Lisa Jackson, and former U.S. Attorney General Eric Holder — the first Black Attorney General of the United States. The discussion centers on racial justice in America, systemic racism, and the historical moment the country was experiencing in summer 2020 following the death of George Floyd and widespread protests.

Holder reflects on what he calls a "generational racial awakening," drawing parallels to previous moments of social change in American history while expressing cautious optimism about the potential for lasting reform. He discusses the interconnected nature of systemic racism across the criminal justice system, housing, employment, education, and environmental policy, arguing that meaningful change requires addressing all of these domains simultaneously.

The conversation also touches on environmental racism, the shared experience of being the first Black leaders of their respective cabinet agencies under President Obama, and how the tech and developer communities can use their skills to advance racial equity and justice.

## Key Topics
- **Generational racial awakening** — Holder frames the 2020 moment as a turning point driven by video evidence and accumulated unease, comparing it to earlier periods of civil rights progress.
- **Systemic racism across institutions** — Discussion of how racism manifests in the criminal justice system, mortgage lending (redlining), Fortune 500 leadership, housing policy, education, and environmental decisions.
- **Police reform** — Specific policy recommendations including banning chokeholds, improved officer training and selection, and federal investigation authority over police departments.
- **Environmental racism** — How communities of color are disproportionately located near pollution sources and face worse health outcomes; infrastructure decisions (e.g., highways through Black neighborhoods) as expressions of systemic bias.
- **Being "firsts"** — Jackson and Holder discuss the dual pressures of being the first Black leaders in their respective positions: obligation to those who sacrificed before them, and the burden of disproving negative stereotypes.
- **Tech community's role** — Holder encourages developers to apply their data aggregation, information sharing, and creative skills to the "racial frontier," making opportunities and civic information more accessible globally.
- **Apple's Racial Equity and Justice Initiative** — Referenced as Apple's commitment to using platforms, voice, and resources to challenge systemic racism.

## Takeaways
- Systemic racism cannot be addressed in isolation — criminal justice reform requires parallel work on housing, employment, education, and environmental justice.
- The tech developer community has a unique opportunity to use data, information sharing, and creative tools to expand access and monitor civic systems across borders.
- Authentic storytelling and willingness to have uncomfortable conversations about race are prerequisites for meaningful change.
- Apple's Racial Equity and Justice Initiative represents a corporate commitment to using institutional power and platforms to challenge inequality.
