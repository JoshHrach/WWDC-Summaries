# The Qualities of Great Design
**WWDC18 · Session 801** · [Watch](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/801/)

_Platforms:_ All Apple platforms (design discipline, no specific SDK)

## Overview
A design evangelist at Apple interviews 13 designers from Apple and the developer community — including type designers, sound designers, motion designers, and app/game developers — to discover what quality means to great designers. Spanning 15 hours of interview footage distilled into core themes, the session covers how designers define quality, why it matters, what aspirations drive their work, and the practical techniques they use daily.

Rather than a traditional technical talk, this session operates as a curated conversation: real, unscripted answers to questions about craftsmanship, care, collaboration, and the experience of designing products that stand the test of time. The interview subjects include collaborators from The Game Band, Layers Conference, Khan Academy, and multiple Apple design disciplines.

## Key Topics

### What Is Quality?
- Quality is "what we agree upon is good" — not random, not slapped together, but considered and intentional
- Quality feels like somebody has already thought of everything the user needs
- Not painful to use in any way — mentally, physically, or emotionally
- Quality recedes: it is about the moment and the person, not the UI or interaction; a well-designed interface is invisible
- Quality is the result of **time, effort, and care** — the hard work shows in the result and makes the outcome more satisfying

### Why Quality Matters
- Quality impacts everyday life across everything people eat, wear, drive, and interact with
- Intentions are felt: if the designer cared, users feel it; if they didn't, that is also felt
- Quality earns **trust** — not from a label, but from every facet of the experience upholding its promise: App Store screenshots, description, responses to reviews, and the interface itself
- Like "being cool," quality must be earned and recognized by others, not claimed

### Four Design Aspirations
1. **Simplicity** — apps should do one thing really well; instantly understandable; real life is where mental energy should go, not figuring out the UI; apps should not be a burden
2. **Stunning** — visual polish means things lining up exactly as intended; swipe animations synchronized with gestures; games should aspire to be works of art and teach rules through active discovery, not tooltips
3. **Timeless** — design for durability ("durability" from Hugo, sound designer); avoid trends that date quickly; ringtones should still feel great after 5–10 years; timeless mindset saves time and effort now
4. **Positive Impact** — the substantial positive effect on the user's life; make life feel easier, more luxurious, more creative; apps that help users take great photos, share moments, or find the right music at the right time

### Six Practical Techniques
1. **Draw caricatures** — when words fail to describe a design problem, exaggerate the perceived flaw in a sketch; this helps others see the issue and builds a "joint gaze" for collaboration
2. **Prototype and try designs in context** — never assume a design works from paper alone; take prototypes out to real users in the real environment where the app is used to answer "why is this good?"
3. **Be open to feedback** — defensiveness blocks progress; after prolonged work, designers develop blind spots; trusted collaborators with fresh eyes reveal what the designer can no longer see
4. **Know what you don't know** — be modest and unassuming when designing for many people; your habits and opinions are not the only ones; ask questions from different perspectives (e.g., someone in a wheelchair, a first-time user, a PC-to-Mac switcher)
5. **Sit with the creative process** — great creative solutions cannot be rushed or hired for; sometimes writer's block requires waiting; patience is a quality of great artists
6. **Set communication expectations upfront** — on new teams, share how you work and how you communicate before starting; ask the same from collaborators; prevents drama and aligns everyone toward the shared goal

## APIs & Frameworks

This session introduces no APIs or SDKs. It is a design philosophy and process session aimed at all Apple platform developers regardless of technical stack. Key design resources referenced:

- **Human Interface Guidelines** — Apple's canonical design reference; defines affordance, touch targets, color, typography, and interaction patterns for all Apple platforms
- **Apple Design Awards** — annual recognition of apps and games that achieve the qualities described in this session
- **Layers Conference** — independent Apple design conference (mentioned as context)

## Code Highlights

No code samples are presented. The session's "implementation" is a set of process questions to internalize:

- What does quality mean to my app specifically? What do I truly care about?
- Have I considered all three phases of interaction (before, during, after)?
- Have I tried this design in context with real users, not just assumed it works?
- Am I being modest — designing for the many lives of my users, not just my own experience?
- Does every facet of the experience (App Store listing, onboarding, UI, error states) uphold the quality promise?

## Takeaways
- Quality is not random — it is crafted, considered, and intentional through every detail; the gaps in the wall panels either line up or they don't.
- Care is a motivator, not a result: caring about both the craft and the user's experience are equally required — caring only about your own taste is not enough.
- Aspire to the four qualities — simplicity, stunning, timeless, positive impact — as ongoing directional aspirations, not binary checkboxes.
- Design is fundamentally collaborative and empathetic: drawing caricatures, prototyping in context, accepting feedback, and practicing modesty are the specific tools that make empathy actionable.
